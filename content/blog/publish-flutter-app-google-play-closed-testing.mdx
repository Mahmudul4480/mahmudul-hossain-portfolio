I'm writing this from Chattogram, a few hours after I hit **Send for review** on Salah Upgrader.

Not production. Not "live on Play." **Closed testing** — Alpha track — with a pile of App content forms filled in and **17 changes** sitting in review. If you're trying to **publish a Flutter app to Google Play closed testing**, this is the paper trail I wish I'd had in one place.

The product story of how we built the app with an agent team is a [separate post](/blog/shipping-flutter-android-app-with-ai-agent-team). This one is about the Play Console side: compliance, signing, store listing, and the choices that keep a worship reminder honest.

## What Salah Upgrader actually is

Salah Upgrader is a free, **ad-free** Flutter Android prayer-reminder utility. Package name: `com.mahmudulhossain.salahupgrader`.

At each prayer time a **full-screen loving reminder** appears — a short message, then two buttons: **accept** (go pray) or **"a little later"** (limited snoozes, then a second reminder near the end of the prayer window). The tone is deliberate. No scare copy. Ever.

Other pieces that matter for the product, not the hype deck:

- **Profession-based, question-style prompts** — the app asks, it doesn't lecture
- A **secret encrypted dua journal**
- Built and iterated on a real **Samsung Galaxy A73**, with full-screen reminder and tune tests on the device

Landing page: [/salah-upgrader](/salah-upgrader). Privacy: [/salah-upgrader/privacy](/salah-upgrader/privacy).

## Why closed testing, not "ship it"

On a personal Play developer account, production eligibility after closed testing still means **12+ Android testers for 14 days**. So the path is: get the release into review → wait for approval → invite testers → hold the closed test long enough → then production.

Right now the status is simple: **changes in review**. No production rollout. Reviews often land within about a week, but I treat that as a hope, not a promise.

Release for this Alpha: **1.0.0**, available to **177 countries + rest of world**, store name **Salah Upgrader: Prayer Alarm**, category **Lifestyle**.

## The compliance checklist that ate the week

Play doesn't care how pretty your Flutter widgets are until the paperwork matches the binary.

### App content and Data safety

I filled the App content questionnaire end to end:

- Privacy policy URL pointed at the live page on this site
- **Data safety**: approximate location, app interactions, diagnostics, and device IDs — **collected, not shared**
- **No ads**
- Target audience **13+**
- Content rating **Everyone / 3+**
- Declared as a **trader**
- Full-screen intent declared as **Alarm clock**
- Exact alarm declared as **Alarm clock**

That last pair is not a clever trick. The reminder is an alarm path. Declaring it that way is how you stay honest with the policy forms.

### The permission I removed on purpose

We had an unused `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE` declaration. Play would have asked for a special-use demo video. The reminder already works through the **alarm + full-screen intent** path, so I removed the unused permission before submission.

Lesson: every declared capability is a question Google can ask. If you don't need it, don't ship it.

### Signing, symbols, analytics

Behind the scenes, before the "Send for review" click:

- Release **upload keystore** created and backed up
- Signed **AAB**
- **NDK 28** with native debug symbols (`SYMBOL_TABLE`) attached
- Production Firebase project + GA4, analytics retention set to **14 months**
- Analytics **advertising ID** on for v1
- Firebase Analytics fires an `onboarding_complete` event when someone finishes first-time setup — **no personal data** in that event

None of that is glamorous. All of it is what "ready for review" actually means.

## Store listing without the hype voice

en-US listing for closed testing:

- Name: **Salah Upgrader: Prayer Alarm**
- Short + full descriptions
- **512** icon
- Feature graphic labeled as AI-generated (Midnight Gold, 1024×500)
- **Five** phone screenshots
- Lifestyle category

Screenshots came from the device — home and prayer times, full-screen reminder, monthly report, reminder settings, wishes, learn. The feature graphic is the one asset I marked as AI so the listing stays transparent.

## A package rename that taught me about encrypted data

We renamed the package before submission. Old encrypted dua rows could not decrypt under the new package key. Early versions of the list hung forever while they tried.

Fix: the list no longer hangs. Migrating users are told to **copy their duas before uninstalling the old app**.

If you encrypt local data keyed to the app identity, treat a package rename as a data-migration event, not a rename-and-forget.

## Why no ads — my choice, not a slogan

I could have put a banner under a prayer reminder. I didn't.

A worship reminder shouldn't monetize attention. That is a personal product decision for Salah Upgrader, not a lecture for every Islamic utility. Free and ad-free means the reminder's job is the reminder — not a funnel into someone else's ad auction.

## What "in review" means for you as a reader

If you found this post looking for how to **publish a Flutter app to Google Play closed testing**, here's the short version of my sequence:

1. Ship a signed AAB with the permissions you actually use
2. Match privacy policy + Data safety to real collection (and say "not shared" only when you mean it)
3. Declare full-screen / exact-alarm use cases accurately
4. Fill store listing assets before you click review
5. Send closed-testing changes — then wait
6. After approval: run the tester period your account type requires before production

Salah Upgrader is **not** live on the public Play Store yet. It is in review for closed testing. When that clears, the next gate is the tester window, not a victory lap.
