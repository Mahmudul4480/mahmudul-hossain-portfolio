A few days after Google Play approved Salah Upgrader for **closed testing**, I still did not have a public app.

The earlier posts covered [shipping with an AI agent team](/blog/shipping-flutter-android-app-with-ai-agent-team) and [getting the Flutter build into closed-testing review](/blog/publish-flutter-app-google-play-closed-testing). This one starts where those stop: what it took to **recruit Google Play closed testing testers** once the track said *Available to selected testers*.

For **newly created personal developer accounts**, Google requires a closed test with at least **12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days** before you can apply for production access. That is Google’s rule, not a slogan — see [App testing requirements for new personal developer accounts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465). Quoting it is easy. Filling it is the work.

## What “approved” looked like

On the morning of **6 November 2026** (Asia/Dhaka), Play Console showed the closed-testing Alpha release as **Available to selected testers**. Production was still inactive.

Two links mattered:

1. Tester Google Group: [groups.google.com/g/salah-upgrader-testers](https://groups.google.com/g/salah-upgrader-testers)
2. Opt-in page: [play.google.com/apps/testing/com.mahmudulhossain.salahupgrader](https://play.google.com/apps/testing/com.mahmudulhossain.salahupgrader)

The public store URL only works for people who are already eligible and opted in. For everyone else it can look missing. That is expected for a closed test, and it confused the first friends who opened the store link cold.

At that first check after approval, **zero** testers had opted in. Approval without people is only a green status.

## The three steps I had to spell out

Every tester needed the **same Gmail** on the phone’s Play Store, then:

1. Join the Google Group  
2. Open the opt-in link and tap **Become a tester**  
3. Install from Google Play

Group membership alone does not count toward Google’s production gate. Play counts **opted-in** testers.

Midweek, the Group and the Console did not always match. On one check the Group looked larger (around ten people including me) while Play still showed **9 testers currently opted-in**; later the opted-in count moved to **10**. I did not audit every missing person, so I will not invent a single cause. What I did change in my invites was practical: send the Group link and the opt-in link together, and say clearly that joining the Group is not the finish line.

## Who I asked for

Salah Upgrader is a free, ad-free prayer reminder. I wanted feedback from people who already live with salah — not a random paid list.

I asked for people who:

- pray the five daily prayers (in jamaat or alone)
- are practising and can give honest product notes
- have an Android phone
- can use the app daily for 14 days
- will not uninstall during that window
- will say in the Google Group what helps, what gets in the way, and what they need

That ask went out on my personal Facebook, LinkedIn, and the Social Media Care page, with the three join steps and a clear line that the app was **not** open to everyone yet.

## Where things stood on 9 November

As of the **9 November 2026** (Asia/Dhaka) Console snapshot I am writing from:

- Production: **Inactive**
- Closed testing release: published
- At least 12 testers opted in: yes
- 12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days: **not yet** — the dashboard said **12 testers have currently been opted in for 1 day**
- **Apply for production** was still unavailable

The Google Group showed **15** members including me. Group size and opted-in count are related, not identical — which is why both need a look.

I am not publishing install numbers here. Play’s statistics and what people told me privately did not always feel like the same picture, and this post does not need a guessed install count.

## Four fixes from closed-test feedback

Closed testing only helps if feedback reaches the product.

As of **9 November 2026**, these four changes were **merged into the app’s private `main` branch**. They were **not yet in the Play closed-testing build**:

1. **Wrong qaza after install.** Prayer times that had already ended before install were being counted as qaza. They are skipped now, and old wrong entries clean up on launch.
2. **Reminders stopping after quiet days.** Alarms are scheduled further ahead (7 days instead of 3), with a resync while the app runs, and a notice the evening before asking the person to open the app once.
3. **Own duas were stuck.** People can edit and delete duas they added (delete asks for confirmation).
4. **Silent save from a notification.** Saving a dua from a notification now shows a confirmation, returns home, and blocks accidental double-saves.

That is the record I want on file when the production questionnaire later asks what testers found and what changed.

## What I would tell another indie

1. Treat approval as the start of recruitment, not the launch.  
2. Put Group + opt-in + “same Gmail on the phone” in one message.  
3. Recruit for the product you are shipping, not only for a headcount.  
4. Check opted-in count and Group count as two separate dials.  
5. Keep a dated list of feedback and fixes — Google asks for that summary when you apply.  
6. Do not announce a public store launch from a closed test.
