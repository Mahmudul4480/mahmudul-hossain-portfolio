I have turned down projects that looked lucrative because the fit was wrong. I have also watched clients burn months and budget on freelancers who could ship a landing page but could not own a database migration at 2am. The difference is not talent — it is whether you asked the right questions before signing.

This is the conversation I wish every client had **before** the kickoff call, written from the side of the engineer who actually writes the code.

## Question 1: "Show me something you shipped end-to-end — not a Figma link"

Portfolio screenshots lie by omission. Ask for:

- A live URL you can click through, including error states
- What backend services power it (database, auth, payments, email)
- What happens when you create two accounts and try to access each other's data

If the answer is only front-end repos or "NDA prevented me from showing work," push for architecture diagrams or anonymized schema exports. Solo ownership means solo accountability across the stack.

## Question 2: "Who writes the SQL migrations?"

Full-stack should mean **full stack**, not "I call Supabase buttons until it works."

Listen for specifics:

- How do they handle rollbacks?
- Do they use Row-Level Security for multi-tenant data?
- Can they explain indexing choices on your core query paths?

A freelancer who outsources database design to a plugin config is fine for MVPs with zero compliance requirements. It is not fine for B2B SaaS handling customer records.

## Question 3: "What is your deployment and incident process?"

Shipping is half the job. Operating is the other half.

Ask directly:

- Where does it deploy (Vercel, Fly, AWS, Railway)?
- How do rollbacks work?
- What monitoring exists beyond "I check it when you email me"?

I document environment variables, run `npm run build` in CI, and leave README handoff notes because clients should not be hostage to tribal knowledge.

## Question 4: "What will you refuse to do?"

This question filters faster than any technical quiz.

Strong solo engineers have boundaries:

- They will not skip auth review to hit a demo date
- They will not copy-paste payment flows from a tutorial without webhook verification
- They will not pretend a no-code tool replaces a data model when scale is the goal

If the answer is "anything you pay for," you are hiring a task rabbit, not an architect.

## Red flags I tell friends to walk away from

- **Cannot explain their last production outage** — everyone has them; evasion means no ownership.
- **Quotes fixed price before scope discovery** — ballpark is fine; fantasy fixed bids become change-order wars.
- **No version control hygiene** — "I will send zip files" in 2026 is a hard no.
- **Mocks agency vocabulary** — "our team" when it is one person and three subcontractors is a transparency problem.

## Green flags worth paying for

- They ask about your **business constraints**, not just feature lists
- They propose a phased roadmap with explicit trade-offs
- They push back on a requirement that creates security debt
- They write docs you can hand to the next developer without embarrassment

## The meta-question: solo vs agency?

Agencies optimize for utilization across a bench. A solo engineer optimizes for reputational survival on every commit — because their next client comes from the last one's referral.

That is why I work solo by choice. When you hire me, you get the person on the architecture call **and** the person fixing the RLS policy. No telephone game.

## Before you send the deposit

Run through this short checklist:

1. Live shipped work verified, not just Dribbble
2. Database and auth ownership confirmed in writing
3. Deployment, monitoring, and rollback discussed
4. Scope document with out-of-scope items explicit
5. Communication cadence agreed (async updates beat surprise meetings)

The goal is not to trap a freelancer in interrogation lights. The goal is to discover whether you are buying a builder who treats your product like theirs — because that is the only model that scales when the agency roster is replaced by one engineer with a GitHub account and something to prove.
